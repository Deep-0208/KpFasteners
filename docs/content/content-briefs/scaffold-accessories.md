### H2 — How the pieces fit together (defensive content wedge)
A short prose block + a system diagram. Names the assembly sequence for a two-face wall shutter:

1. Two shutter faces (plywood on steel frame) set to design thickness.
2. Tie rod passed through the shutter and through both walers.
3. Waller plate slipped onto each end of the rod against the waler.
4. Wing nut spun on and torqued against the waller plate to draw the shutter faces together.
5. After concrete cures: wing nuts released, tie rod withdrawn (or snapped-off at the debond zone for loop ties). The rod is either recovered for reuse or left cast-in with a water-stop disc for water-retaining walls.

This section is what a contractor's site engineer will screenshot. It is also the passage AI Overviews will cite for *"how does a tie-rod formwork tie work"* — long-tail informational leak into commercial intent.

## 5. FAQs (draft — 5 questions, 2-3 sentence answers)

**Q1: What is the difference between a D15 and a D20 tie rod?**
D15 has a nominal ~15 mm diameter and is the workhorse tie for standard wall and column shutters. D20 (~20 mm) is used where higher pressure or wider walls require higher safe working load — commonly on bridge piers, retaining walls, and heavier precast forms. Wing nuts and waller plates come in matching sizes; pieces are not interchangeable between D15 and D20 systems.

**Q2: Do you supply hot-dip galvanised waller plates and wing nuts?**
Yes — HDG per IS 2629 / ISO 1461 is the standard finish for repeat-use waller plates and wing nuts, because these pieces are stored on-site through monsoons and cycles of pour-and-strip. Tie rods themselves are commonly supplied self-colour (black) when they will be recovered from a formed pour. `[CLIENT TO CONFIRM whether HDG is done in-house or through a partner galvaniser.]`

**Q4: Can I order a system-specific fit (PERI, Doka, MEVA)?**
