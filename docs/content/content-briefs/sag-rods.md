## 3. Content outline (H1 → H3, ~1,200–1,500 words target)

### H1
`Sag Rods Manufacturer — Purlin Bracing & Solar Racking`

### H2 — What a sag rod actually does
*(One tight paragraph. A sag rod is a tension member that keeps a light-gauge purlin from twisting under one-sided sheeting load and from sagging over its own weight before the roof sheet is fixed. Named for the deflection it prevents. Distinguishes it from tie rods (compression / turnbuckle assemblies) and stud bolts (short, fully threaded, flange-clamp use).)*

### H2 — Where sag rods are used
- **PEB roof purlin bracing** — the classic case: one, two or three lines of sag rods across the span, splitting the purlin length into bays. Anchor at each rafter is critical.
- **PEB wall girt bracing** — same idea, applied to sidewall girts.
- **Solar racking cross-bracing** — table-to-table diagonal, and torsion-tube-to-post bracing, especially on tracker structures.
- **Structural steel** — bridge deck and industrial-mezzanine tension members where a rod-and-nut assembly is preferred over an angle brace.

### H2 — Materials & standards
- **Base steel:** Mild steel rounds; high-tensile grades (e.g., Grade 8.8 equivalent) on request.
- **Standards touched:** No dedicated Indian standard for "sag rod" as a finished part — spec is normally by base-material grade (IS 3757 / ASTM A36 / IS 1367 for property class) and by thread standard (metric 6g / UNC 2A). PEB fabricators typically specify by rod diameter, thread pitch, and coating class.
- **Coatings:** Hot-dip galvanised (HDG) for outdoor exposure is the default — roof and solar applications both live outside. Zinc electroplate is available for indoor / short-service applications.

### H2 — Threading & end preparation
- **Threading pattern options:** Both ends threaded (typical), one end threaded, full-length threaded (rare, used when the field crew wants adjustment freedom).
- **Thread length at each end:** Typically 75–150 mm; specify on the RFQ.
- **End-fit:** Hex nut + washer at each end; optional coupler for site splice on long spans.

### H2 — How to specify a sag rod (procurement primer)
*(3 short paragraphs. The rod is defined by five things — diameter, total length, thread pitch, thread-length-at-each-end, coating class. A quick worked example: "For a Z-purlin at 6 m span, a single-line M16 HDG sag rod with 100 mm thread at each end and matching hex nut + flat washer is the common answer.")*
